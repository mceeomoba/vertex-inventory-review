Your product import deleted images: what row order has to do with it.

Open the free Product CSV Preflight

Shopify warns that sorting a product CSV in a spreadsheet program can separate products from their image links and lose images. Keep your import file in its original order, and sort only a throwaway copy.

You edited a product CSV, maybe sorted it to group items for review, imported it, and now product images are gone. A re-import isn't fixing it. Here is what the documentation says, and how to keep it from happening again.

Why order matters

A product CSV is positional. A product's first row carries its title and product-level fields, and additional image rows reuse the product's handle, one row per image. Shopify's own import guide cautions that importing a CSV file that has been sorted by a spreadsheet editor such as Microsoft Excel or Apple Numbers might cause products to be removed from their relevant image links on the CSV, and that the product's images will be lost. Its product CSV guide also notes that image URLs are used only during the import process.

Two more official facts make this high-stakes:

  • Shopify says product imports started with a CSV file can't be canceled once they begin, and there is no import history view; you review recent changes in the store's activity log.
  • Shopify tells you to have a backup of your product data before you start an import.

If it already happened

  1. Stop importing. Repeated imports with a damaged or re-sorted file can compound the changes. Work from copies.
  2. Find your last good export or backup. If you exported before the import, that file plus your original image files is your reference for what was attached where.
  3. Check your image URLs. Shopify requires publicly accessible direct image URLs (https://, no password protection). Its troubleshooting docs say a mistake in the image URL causes a download validation error, and that removing products or images from the store after exporting, then importing without updating the URLs, makes the import fail. If the products were deleted and re-created, old product-image URLs may no longer point anywhere useful.
  4. Rebuild in small, verifiable pieces. Fix a handful of products first, with correct handles and working image URLs, and confirm the result before scaling up. Shopify's import guide recommends testing a small subset first for large imports. Shopify Support is the documented route when the listed fixes don't cover your case.
  5. Record what happened. Note the file, the sort operation and the time. That record matters if you ask support about recovery, and it makes the cause checkable later.

Be skeptical of forum recovery tricks, including claims that deleted images keep living at special URLs. They are unverified and may not apply to your store or to current behavior.

Before your next import

  1. Export fresh and keep that file untouched. It is both your backup and your reference for the original row order.
  2. Never sort the file you will import. If sorting helps you review, sort a second copy and mark it clearly as review-only. Import the file that preserves the original structure.
  3. Edit narrowly. Delete no columns and reorder nothing you don't have to. Review blanks with the overwrite behavior in mind: included blank cells can clear stored values.
  4. Check image rows before import. Image rows carry the product's handle, one row per image, up to 250 images per product. Keep them attached to the right product group, in order.
  5. Run a structural second check. The free Vertex Product CSV Preflight reviews your file in your browser and flags structural issues; it treats continuation and image rows with blank titles as normal rather than errors to "clean up." Read its findings next to your original export.
  6. Test small first. Import a few products, confirm images and fields, then scale.
Fictional example: A merchant sorts a 9,000-row product CSV by price to review price edits, saves, and imports that sorted file. The import completes with errors, and thousands of product images are gone from the storefront. The prices were the only intended change; the sort silently changed which rows carried which image links. Restoring means rebuilding image associations from a pre-import export and the original image files, in small tested batches. Fictional illustration, not a customer result.
Tool limit: The preflight does not validate image URLs, check your store, or restore anything. It cannot detect that a file was sorted. Image review stays manual; the tool is a second set of eyes on structure, not import approval.
